Polynomials: Introduction and Degrees

Welcome! In this activity, we'll explore polynomials - one of the most important concepts in algebra. Let's begin with understanding what polynomials are and their degrees!

What is a Polynomial?

A polynomial in x is an expression of the form:

aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + ... + a₁x + a₀

Where:

  • a₀, a₁, ... aₙ are real numbers (called coefficients)
  • n is a non-negative integer (0, 1, 2, 3, ...)
  • The variable x has only non-negative integer powers

Polynomials are fundamental in algebra and appear in many mathematical contexts, from simple equations to complex mathematical models.

Degree of a Polynomial

The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable in the polynomial.

To find the degree:

  1. Look at all the terms in the polynomial
  2. Identify the power (exponent) of the variable in each term
  3. The largest of these powers is the degree

Example 1: Linear Polynomial

Polynomial: 4x + 2

Terms: 4x (power = 1), 2 (power = 0, constant term)
Highest power: 1
Degree: 1

Example 2: Quadratic Polynomial

Polynomial: 2y² - 3y + 4

Terms: 2y² (power = 2), -3y (power = 1), 4 (power = 0)
Highest power: 2
Degree: 2

Example 3: Cubic Polynomial

Polynomial: 5x³ - 4x² + x - 2

Terms: 5x³ (power = 3), -4x² (power = 2), x (power = 1), -2 (power = 0)
Highest power: 3
Degree: 3

What is NOT a Polynomial?

Important: Not all expressions with variables are polynomials!

Key Rule

In a polynomial, all powers of the variable must be non-negative integers (0, 1, 2, 3, ...). If an expression has negative or fractional powers, it is NOT a polynomial.

Examples of Non-Polynomials

  • 1/x = x⁻¹ - has a negative power (-1)
  • √x = x¹/² - has a fractional power (1/2)
  • 1/(x² + 2x + 3) - has a negative power in the denominator
  • x + 1/x - contains x⁻¹, so it's not a polynomial

These expressions are valid mathematical expressions, but they are not polynomials because they violate the rule that all powers must be non-negative integers.

Special Case: Constant Polynomials

A constant number like 5 or -3 IS a polynomial! It's called a constant polynomial.

We can write 5 as 5x⁰ = 5, so it has degree 0.

Examples: 5, -3, 0, 100 are all constant polynomials with degree 0.

Finding the Degree: More Examples

Example: Finding Degree of 7u⁶ - 3u⁴ + 2u² - 4u + 8

Step 1: Identify all terms and their powers:

  • 7u⁶ → power = 6
  • -3u⁴ → power = 4
  • 2u² → power = 2
  • -4u → power = 1
  • 8 → power = 0 (constant term)

Step 2: Find the highest power: 6

Answer: The degree of the polynomial is 6

Remember: We always look for the term with the highest exponent, not the number of terms or the value of any constant!

Identifying Polynomials

To identify whether an expression is a polynomial, check that all powers of the variable are whole numbers (0, 1, 2, 3, ...).

Example: Which is NOT a polynomial?

Question: Which of the following is NOT a polynomial?

Answer: x + 1/x

Explanation: The term 1/x = x⁻¹ has a negative exponent (-1). In a polynomial, all exponents of variables must be non-negative integers. Therefore, x + 1/x is not a polynomial.

Other options are polynomials:

  • x³ - 4x - IS a polynomial (powers are 3 and 1)
  • 5 - IS a polynomial (constant polynomial, degree 0)
  • x² + 2x + 1 - IS a polynomial (powers are 2, 1, and 0)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Confusing Coefficient with Degree

Wrong: Thinking the constant term (like 8) is the degree

Correct: The degree is the highest exponent of the variable, not the value of any coefficient or constant. For 7u⁶ - 3u⁴ + 2u² - 4u + 8, the degree is 6 (from u⁶), not 8 (the constant).

Mistake 2: Counting Terms Instead of Finding Highest Power

Wrong: Thinking the number of terms determines the degree

Correct: The degree is about the highest power of the variable, not how many terms exist. A polynomial with 5 terms can have degree 6, 2, or any number depending on the highest exponent.

Mistake 3: Not Checking All Terms

Wrong: Looking at only one term and assuming it's the highest

Correct: You must check every term in the polynomial to find which has the largest exponent. Don't assume the first term has the highest power!

Mistake 4: Thinking Constants Are Not Polynomials

Wrong: Believing that a number like 5 is not a polynomial

Correct: A constant number IS a polynomial! It's called a constant polynomial with degree 0. We can write 5 as 5x⁰ = 5.

Key Points to Remember

  • A polynomial in x has the form: aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + ... + a₁x + a₀
  • All powers of the variable must be non-negative integers (0, 1, 2, 3, ...)
  • The degree is the highest power of the variable
  • To find the degree:
    • Look at all terms in the polynomial
    • Identify the power of the variable in each term
    • The largest power is the degree
  • Not polynomials: Expressions with negative powers (like 1/x = x⁻¹) or fractional powers (like √x = x¹/²)
  • Constant polynomials: Numbers like 5, -3 are polynomials with degree 0
  • Examples:
    • 4x + 2 → degree 1
    • 2y² - 3y + 4 → degree 2
    • 5x³ - 4x² + x - 2 → degree 3
    • 7u⁶ - 3u⁴ + 2u² - 4u + 8 → degree 6
