The Making of a Global World: Introduction to the Pre-modern World

Welcome! When we talk of 'GLOBALISATION', we often think of something that emerged in the last 50 years. But as you'll discover, the making of the global world has a LONG HISTORY – of trade, migration, movement of capital, and much more. Let's explore how the world became interconnected long before modern times.

The World Was Always Connected

All through history, human societies have become steadily more INTERLINKED. The connections we see today have deep historical roots.

Who Travelled and Why?

From ancient times, people travelled vast distances for various reasons:

  • TRAVELLERS seeking knowledge
  • TRADERS looking for profit
  • PRIESTS and PILGRIMS for spiritual fulfilment
  • Those escaping PERSECUTION

What Did They Carry With Them?

These travellers didn't just move themselves – they carried many things that connected distant parts of the world:

  • GOODS and MONEY - Trade items and currencies
  • VALUES and SKILLS - Cultural practices and knowledge
  • IDEAS and INVENTIONS - New technologies and concepts
  • GERMS and DISEASES - Unfortunately, diseases also spread

These movements created connections between distant parts of the world, long before modern transportation and communication.

Ancient Evidence of Global Connections

History provides fascinating examples of how early these connections began. The evidence shows that globalisation is not a recent phenomenon!

1. Indus Valley Trade (3000 BCE)

Evidence: An active COASTAL TRADE linked the Indus valley civilisations with present-day West Asia.

Significance: This is more than 5,000 years ago! It shows that trade networks existed in ancient times, connecting different regions through maritime routes.

2. Cowries as Currency

What are cowries? Cowries (also called cowdi) are seashells that were used as a form of currency in ancient times.

Trade network: From the MALDIVES, cowries found their way to:

  • CHINA in the east
  • EAST AFRICA in the west

Duration: This trade lasted for MORE THAN A THOUSAND YEARS (millennia), showing the stability and importance of these ancient trade networks.

3. Spread of Diseases

Early evidence: Long-distance spread of disease-carrying germs can be traced to the SEVENTH CENTURY.

Clear connections: By the THIRTEENTH CENTURY, this had become an unmistakable link between regions.

Significance: The spread of diseases shows how interconnected different regions were, even in ancient times. Unfortunately, this was one negative consequence of increased global connections.

So the 'global world' is not new – its roots are ancient! The making of the global world happened through multiple phases over centuries.

Why Study Pre-modern Globalisation?

Understanding the history of globalisation helps us see important patterns and processes:

1. Globalisation is a Process

It didn't happen overnight. Globalisation developed over centuries through many phases, each building on previous connections and creating new ones.

2. Different Forces Drove It

Multiple factors contributed to the making of the global world:

  • Trade and commerce - Economic exchanges
  • Religion and culture - Spread of beliefs and practices
  • Migration and settlement - Movement of people
  • Conquest and colonisation - Political expansion

3. Consequences Were Mixed

Global connections had both positive and negative effects:

  • Positive: Exchange of valuable goods and ideas
  • Negative: Spread of diseases
  • Mixed: Both opportunities and exploitation

In this chapter, we'll focus on the PRE-MODERN WORLD – the era BEFORE the Industrial Revolution transformed global connections in new ways.

Understanding Through Examples

Question: According to the textbook, what items did ancient travellers carry with them across vast distances?

Explanation: The textbook specifically states that travellers 'carried goods, money, values, skills, ideas, inventions, and even germs and diseases.' This shows how comprehensive ancient exchanges were - not just material goods, but also knowledge, culture, and unfortunately, diseases. This comprehensive exchange is what made the world interconnected.

Question: What evidence does the textbook give for ancient global connections involving the Indus Valley?

Answer: An active coastal trade linked Indus Valley with West Asia as early as 3000 BCE

Explanation: The textbook specifically states: 'As early as 3000 BCE an active coastal trade linked the Indus valley civilisations with present-day West Asia.' Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Thinking Globalisation is Recent

Wrong: Believing globalisation only started in the last 50 years

Correct: Globalisation has a long history. Evidence shows trade connections from 3000 BCE, and the spread of ideas, goods, and diseases has been happening for thousands of years.

Mistake 2: Limiting Ancient Exchanges to Goods

Wrong: Thinking ancient travellers only carried luxury goods like silk and spices

Correct: Ancient travellers carried much more: goods, money, values, skills, ideas, inventions, and even germs and diseases. The exchanges were comprehensive and multi-dimensional.

Mistake 3: Confusing Time Periods

Wrong: Thinking trade began only after European arrival or that modern technology existed in ancient times

Correct: Indus Valley trade existed from 3000 BCE, thousands of years before European arrival. The pre-modern world had its own technologies and trade networks, different from modern ones.
